This part is a side project. Most of the posts so far were about getting the original game to
work in Godot. This one is about making it look a bit newer without losing the original.

Same rule as the effects in [Part 8]({{ "/part-8/" | relative_url }}): everything here is an
option. The options window has a "Trees" row and a "Grass" row, each with Legacy and New.
Legacy is the exact original client. New is what this post is about. Both switch live, the
world does not reload.

The HD textures from [Part 9]({{ "/part-9/" | relative_url }}) got the same option, a
"Textures" row. So in the comparisons below, Legacy means everything original: trees, grass
and textures. New means everything new.

![A path next to Talking Island village with the New trees and grass]({{ "/assets/img/hero-part10.jpg" | relative_url }})

## Trees, first attempt

An Interlude tree is a trunk plus a few big flat planes with a leaf texture on them. From far
away that works. Up close you see the cards.

My first idea was to keep the original tree and only rebuild the crown. New leaf cards placed
on top of the original leaf planes, 3D branches inside, and real leaf photos (CC0 scans from
ambientCG and Poly Haven) cut into twigs. I got it working for 72 trees.

![Original tree (left of each pair) and the rebuilt crown (right)]({{ "/assets/img/trees-crowns-pairs.jpg" | relative_url }})

It did not work. The crowns came out round and soft, like green balls on a stick, and the
branches looked fake. After a couple of days of tuning I dropped it.

## Trees, real models

The second idea was simpler: replace the whole tree with a real 3D model. I searched
Sketchfab for free models with a CC-BY license and ended up with 22 tree models from 17
uploads: oak, elm, beech, birch, maple, acacia, willow, pine, spruce, fir, juniper, two palms,
a bald cypress, a few gnarly old trees and three dead oaks. The credits are at the end.

Before anything goes into the game I render the models side by side in Blender and check them.

![The first batch of models in Blender, before export]({{ "/assets/img/tree-models-blender.jpg" | relative_url }})

Then every tree mesh in the game gets a decision. 152 tree meshes get a model, and they are
placed 29,181 times across the map. 82 meshes stay Legacy on purpose: bushes, ferns, fantasy
trees with multi-colour leaves, ruins. Nothing on Sketchfab looks like them, and a wrong tree
is worse than an old one.

To make a model fit in place of the original:

- **Size and position.** The model goes on the original trunk base and is scaled to the
  original tree's height. The width follows the original too, but only between 0.75x and
  1.35x, so a model never looks stretched.
- **Variety.** Each placed tree picks a model variant and a rotation from a hash of its world
  position. The same spot always gets the same tree, but two trees next to each other do not
  look like copies.
- **Colour.** L2 has green trees, orange autumn trees, snowy trees, dark swamp trees. I wanted
  one oak model to work for all of them. Multiplying the model texture by the original colour
  gave mud (green times orange). What works is to keep only the brightness of the model
  texture and colour it with the original tree's leaf or bark colour:

```glsl
vec4 tex = texture(albedo_tex, UV);
vec3 tint = is_leaf ? leaf_tint * leaf_brightness : bark_tint;
float lum = dot(tex.rgb, vec3(0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722));
vec3 recoloured = tint * min(lum / max(mean_lum, 0.005), 3.0);
```

`leaf_tint` and `bark_tint` are set per tree, measured from the original tree's textures.
`mean_lum` is the average brightness of the model texture, so the leaf detail keeps its
contrast.

- **Matte.** The Sketchfab leaves were single sided and a bit metallic. In the game they are
  double sided with no specular, like the rest of the port. They also sway a little in the
  wind.

## Dead trees

Not every tree is green. At the Execution Grounds the original trees are slender and twisted,
with a few withered olive leaves. For them I used a set of three dead English oaks. The result
looks more dead than the original, which still has some leaves. Not a perfect match, but much
closer than putting a green oak there.

![Execution Grounds, Legacy and New]({{ "/assets/img/execution-grounds-legacy-new.jpg" | relative_url }})

## Floating roots

Some models have big roots. The original L2 trees stand on a thin trunk, so on a slope the new
tree's roots hang in the air on the downhill side. On the Execution Grounds tile, 186 of 676
trees were floating.

The fix has two parts. First, each model is pushed down until its root tips are 5 cm under
the terrain, but never more than 6% of the tree height, or the branches get buried on steep
slopes:

```gdscript
var worst := -INF
for v in _footprint(node.mesh):
    var p := xf * v
    worst = maxf(worst, p.y - _ground_height(g, p.x, p.z))
var tall := (xf * Vector3.UP).y - base.y
var sink := clampf(worst + GROUND_SINK, 0.0, GROUND_MAX_SINK * tall)
```

`_footprint` is the outermost bark vertex near the bottom of the model, in 24 directions
around the trunk. Those are the root tips that hang first on a slope.

Second, for the models with the biggest roots I cut the roots off in Blender at 5% of the
height, so the trunk just goes into the ground. After both, 10 trees on that tile still float
a little, all on the steepest slopes and less than half a metre.

## Grass

The original ground is a flat texture with some small plants on top, herbs and flowers. The
New grass is a carpet of real grass blades on top of that. The original plants stay.

![Grass, Legacy and New. The trees stay Legacy in both, so only the grass and the textures change.]({{ "/assets/img/grass-legacy-new.jpg" | relative_url }})

It is built at runtime in 16 m chunks around the camera, up to 150 m, where the fog is solid.
For each chunk it reads the tile's splat map (which texture is where), the terrain height,
the holes (cave entrances), the props and the water, so there is no grass inside a house or
under a lake. Close blades are real geometry. Past 35 m each patch becomes a few crossed
cards, which from that far look the same.

The hard part was deciding where grass goes and what colour it is. The map uses 353 terrain
textures. I made contact sheets of all of them and sorted each one by hand: green grass, dry
grass, or bare (dirt, sand, road, rock, snow). Some get a lower density so they become clumps
on soil instead of a lawn. In the end 176 textures get grass, 122 green and 54 dry. The blade
colour comes from the ground texture itself, so grass on dark ground is dark and grass on
yellow ground is yellow:

```json
"ag02":  {"type": "green", "density": 1,   "color": [0.4485, 0.553, 0.1781]},
"ag05":  {"type": "dry",   "density": 1,   "color": [0.4668, 0.3709, 0.1873]},
"ac_07": {"type": "green", "density": 0.6, "color": [0.2689, 0.3091, 0.1445]}
```

Dry ground gets dry blades with a different texture. You can see it in the Execution Grounds
shot above, the brown ground has straw coloured grass.

## Performance

When I first walked around the Execution Grounds with the grass on, I had about 60 fps.
Without it, 90. So I wrote a small bench: it loads the 4 tiles around a spot, uses
the same fog, lights and global illumination as the game, orbits a camera like the game camera
does, and measures Legacy against New.

Two things came out of it:

- **Trees past the fog.** The fog is solid at 150 m, but the camera draws up to 1000 m and the
  sky still shows through the fog a little. So far away trees were still full models, drawn
  as flat fog coloured shapes. Now a model is drawn only up to 150 m. Past that the original L2
  tree, a few hundred triangles, stands in.
- **Grass was CPU, not GPU.** I expected the blades to be the cost. They were not. Drawing the
  whole carpet was about 0.4 ms of GPU time. The problem was my own script: every frame it
  checked about 1,400 chunk slots using string keys and re-picked the distance level for 320
  chunks, around 3 ms per frame. Now it does that only after the camera moves 2 m or a tile
  loads. With the grass on, the bench went from 110 fps to 83 before the fix. After it, the
  grass costs about 8% of the frame instead of 25%.
